Create THE LAST COLOR, a 15-second emotional micro-story in exactly five 3-second takes. The supplied afro photo is a turnaround of ONE man, not a group: preserve his face, huge round dark curly afro, round gold glasses, gold hoops, moustache and short beard, brown patterned three-piece suit, white shirt and brown tie. One character, same outfit throughout. References define identity, not a fixed pose or beige studio.
Rain-soaked almost-desaturated stone city, sparse people absent except the protagonist. Warm amber is the only initial saturated color. Later a purposeful move into a small grey courtyard is part of the story, not a continuity error. Quiet patient camera, no flashy cutting.
1: Medium side view in the grey street. The man notices one tiny amber glowing pigment fleck on a stone wall. His gaze settles on it, no touching yet.
2: Genuine hand detail. His fingertip touches the already visible amber fleck once; the pigment/light transfers onto his finger. The original wall spot dims. End with amber on fingertip, no spread across the city yet.
3: Wider rear three-quarter arrival in a small grey courtyard, same costume, amber fingertip held carefully near his chest. He takes a short step toward a desaturated climbing vine on the courtyard wall. The color is already held, do not rediscover or touch the original fleck.
4: Close side action. His amber fingertip touches one grey vine leaf. Warm green and gold slowly spread along the first few leaves and into the wall's reflected light. Single touch, no instant whole-world flash. Keep the color source on the hand legible.
5: Wide courtyard payoff. Vine already partly colored; the warm transformation spreads gently through the rest of the courtyard while distant street remains grey. Man lowers hand and watches. End held in a newly warm place, not a repeated hand touch or hero product pose. Each shot is one independent generated take and performs only its own event once. Write a self-contained prompt and first-frame image_prompt BEFORE that shot's new action. Inherited prop state is already established; never replay earlier events or summarize the whole film in each shot. Use SHOT_001 etc in chronological order. Preserve stated product/character design and motivated continuity, not identical framing. Essential action must happen within the usable shot duration, then hold its consequence for unused raw-take time. Protect key actions, no internal sub-cuts through them. Generate clean footage without lettering, logos, subtitles or graphic stickers. Editorial titles and vector accents are supplied separately: do not add fx.text, text objects, motion_title, motion_cues or text layers. Add no extra generated shots. No source video and no generated scene-illustration prepass. Music accents are intent; finishing uses measured attacks, never reorder the story for an assumed drop. Music direction: Instrumental emotive ambient electronica, spacious 84 BPM pulse, fragile glassy motif, warm low synth bed, sparse soft percussion. Begin desaturated and intimate, gradually broaden harmonic warmth as color is shared, gentle sustained hopeful ending. No dramatic trailer swell, jazz, swing, sax, vocals or mandatory bass drop.

04You stay the director

A first cut, not a final answer.

Six things HyperKut does so that you can steer the edit instead of re-rolling it. Each card says how it works and what it saves.

Direct

Regenerate one shot

Render only what you changed.

Mechanism

Change a shot's prompt and send only that shot back to ComfyUI. Locked shots and every other take stay exactly as they are.

Why it helps

Fixing one moment of a long edit does not mean rendering the whole edit again.

1 of 28shots re-rendered to fix one, in the fashion Remix
Takes

Keep every take

Try a variation, lose nothing.

Mechanism

Each regeneration adds a take to its shot. You pick the active take on the timeline and can swap back at any time.

Why it helps

You can explore a different version of a shot without giving up the one you liked.

0 rendersto switch the active take
Edit

Trim and reorder after

Retime the edit, not the model.

Mechanism

Trim windows, cuts and the order of shots live in a timeline you edit after generation, then you export the final video.

Why it helps

Pace and order are decided on real footage, with no new generation in the loop.

No new renderto trim, cut or reorder shots, then export
Remix

Read the reference

Keep what made the edit work.

Mechanism

The cuts, framing and rhythm are read from the reference video. Every shot is then redrawn with your subject.

Why it helps

You inherit the pacing of an edit you already like, with your own character in it.

28 shotsread from a 12.2 s reference edit
Music

Cut on your beats

The edit moves with the track.

Mechanism

The beats and energy of your track set the length of each shot. The music itself is never touched.

Why it helps

Cuts land where the track hits, without hand-placing a single marker.

4 shotsof 3.9, 2.9, 2.9 and 2.4 s, set by the beats of a 12 s track
Text

A local planner

Gemma 4 writes the shot list.

Mechanism

A local model turns your idea into a shot list with one prompt per shot. You can edit each prompt before or after it renders.

Why it helps

You start from a readable plan instead of a black box.

05Open by default

Open models. Open code.

Every job is a ComfyUI workflow you can open, read and change.

Open-weights models

MiniMax H3 renders the shots, Gemma 4 plans them, YuE2 writes the music and FLUX.2 klein draws optional location plates. Each model keeps its own license: see LICENSES.md.

Every mode is a ComfyUI workflow

The Studio fills a ComfyUI workflow for each job. The workflows are in the repo, built from Majoor VideoTools nodes, so you can open and change them.

Comfy developer platform

Jobs go through the official Comfy SDK to the Comfy API, served locally by comfy-api-proxy in front of your ComfyUI. Progress and previews stream back to the browser.

MIT code

The Studio is open source under the MIT license. Read it, change it, run it.
